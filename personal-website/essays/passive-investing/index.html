Passive Investing Only Works Because Most People Won't Do It

PS
Pranit Saindani
Former banking regulator, RBI · BNP Paribas · Nasdaq
Aug 5, 2026
6 min read
featured image

If everyone actually followed the advice to "just buy the index," passive investing would stop working within a generation. It survives precisely because most people won't discipline themselves to do it, and even fewer will do it consistently through a crash.

That's not an argument against index funds. It's a warning about what the advice actually depends on, which almost nobody explains when they give it.

The consensus everyone repeats

The pitch is simple and mostly correct: low-cost index funds beat the majority of actively managed funds over long horizons, after fees. Buy the whole market, stop trying to pick winners, let compounding do the work. It's good advice. I'd give it to almost anyone starting out.

But the pitch is usually delivered as if it were a law of markets rather than a byproduct of how markets are currently structured.

The turn

Index funds don't do price discovery. They free-ride on it. Someone still has to research companies, judge earnings against expectations, and buy or sell based on that judgment, because that's what keeps prices roughly tethered to the value of the businesses underneath them. Active managers do that work, badly on average, but they do it. Passive capital just buys a slice of whatever active capital has already priced.

Passive investing is a strategy that works because it stays a minority strategy.

As passive flows grow relative to active flows, that price-discovery function gets thinner. Index funds buy proportionally to market capitalization regardless of whether a company is cheap or expensive relative to its earnings. That means growing passive share doesn't just track existing mispricings, it can amplify them, pushing more capital toward whatever is already large rather than whatever is actually undervalued. Nobody has to hit a precise saturation point for this to matter. The distortion grows at the margin, quietly, long before anyone agrees the tipping point has arrived.

None of this means indexing is broken today. It means the "just buy the index and never think about it again" advice quietly assumes a world where enough other people are still doing the thinking for you.

What actually matters for you

The bigger reason passive investing works for the individual investor has almost nothing to do with market structure. It's behavioral. The real value of "buy the index and hold" isn't the strategy's cleverness, it's that it removes your own worst instincts from the equation: the urge to sell in a panic, chase last year's winner, or time a re-entry you'll never get right.

Most people who abandon index investing don't abandon it because the strategy failed. They abandon it because they couldn't tolerate watching it fall 30% without doing something. So evaluate yourself before you evaluate the fund. The question that determines whether passive investing will work for you isn't about expense ratios. It's whether you can sit still.
