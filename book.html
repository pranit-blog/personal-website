Why this book

Most financial advice solves the wrong problem

Spend less than you earn. Automate your savings. Buy the index fund and leave it alone. None of that is wrong. It's also nearly useless on its own, because knowing what to do with money and actually doing it are two completely different skills.

Almost nobody teaches the second one.

I spent a decade inside the institutions that write the rules for how money is supposed to move: the Reserve Bank of India, BNP Paribas, Nasdaq. What stuck with me wasn't the spreadsheets. It was how predictably people, including the professionals, act against their own stated interests the moment the right trigger hits: a notification, a sale, a bad day, a friend's new car.

Mindless Money explores the psychology behind the financial decisions we make every day: impulse purchases, lifestyle inflation, credit cards, social pressure, emotional spending, and the small habits that quietly run our financial lives without asking permission first.

What you'll walk away with

Awareness

Spot the trigger before it spots you

Recognize the psychological cues behind your own spending, in the moment, not after the statement arrives.

Behaviour

Understand the forces you're up against

See how emotion, habit, and social pressure shape money decisions long before logic gets a vote.

Traps

Avoid the common behavioural traps

Credit cards, debt, and investing are designed to exploit predictable weaknesses. Learn to see the design.

Systems

Design decisions you don't have to make

Build simple defaults and systems that make the better choice the easier one, instead of relying on willpower.

Habits

Build healthier habits, not stricter budgets

This isn't about becoming perfect with money. It's about noticing the decisions you're already making.

How it works

A workbook, not a lecture

Most money books tell you to stop buying coffee. This one doesn't, because that was never really the problem. Each chapter pairs institutional insight from banking and regulation with an interactive exercise or scenario, a "game," you can run against your own financial life immediately.

It's built to be worked through, not just read. 123 pages, no filler, designed for a few focused sittings rather than a shelf you mean to get back to.
