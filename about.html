I'm Pranit Saindani. For ten years I worked inside the machinery that decides which banks stay open, which trades get flagged, and which risks get written off as acceptable. Reserve Bank of India, BNP Paribas, Nasdaq — banking supervision, regulatory policy, compliance, supervisory audits. If you've ever wondered how the sausage of "financial stability" actually gets made, I was in the room.

That's not a resume flex. It's the reason I write the way I write. Most commentary on finance and technology comes from people watching the system from the outside, reverse-engineering motives from headlines. I've seen the internal memos, sat in the escalation meetings, and watched good rules produce bad outcomes because nobody stress-tested the incentives underneath them. Credibility, for me, comes from having worked inside the systems I now critique — not from academic distance.

I hold an MBA in Finance from IIM Indore, which taught me the theory. The regulatory seats taught me where the theory breaks. Both matter, but only one of them shows up in a crisis.

These days I'm building Hilio, a financial wellbeing app that treats money as an emotional problem before it's a math problem — because a decade of supervising institutions convinced me that most financial failure, personal or institutional, starts with a story people tell themselves, not a spreadsheet.

This site is where I write about all of it: the regulation that doesn't regulate, the technology that reshapes behaviour faster than anyone admits, and the financial habits we defend long after they've stopped making sense. First-principles thinking, aimed at an educated reader who doesn't need everything explained twice.
